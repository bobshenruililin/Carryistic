# Bead camera test

Carry’s photograph of the hanging glass strand and the beaded base in *To Bleed in Liquid Gold* supplied the single reference image, P08-03. The experiment asks whether moving deeper into one bead can prepare the basketball cut. [Source post and slide](https://www.instagram.com/20.09.07.29/p/DbwiQZjjIL4/?hl=en&img_index=3)

## Result

**Version A is the film cut.** Across its 33 frames, the camera travels down the source photograph. The heaped beads grow to fill the frame, the four metallic taps settle, and the isolated orange ball drops into the same field of red.

I compared two equal-length pieces of the generated shot in the same position, between the artist’s sculpture video and the basketball. Frames 0–32 preserve the support, gold underside and bead pile. The pickup stays wide through the last tap. In frames 64–96, the depth of field convincingly brings the garnet facets forward. The camera settles on two hanging beads at screen right with the pile behind them. The amber reflection stays inside one facet. Cutting here shifts the viewer’s gaze across the frame just as the ball enters near the top center. The descending move in A gives the picture and the percussion one direction.
