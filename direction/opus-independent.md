# Opus · independent first look

Written after opening the 15 contact sheets, the original post folders and frame grids from the three MP4s, and **before** reading `direction/director-record.md`, `artist-reading.md`, `collaboration.md` or the film.

**Contamination note.** My first command printed the README, so I already knew the director’s film goes from Fly’s four blocks to the red cap and ends on the number 30 jersey. For that reason I picked a relationship the README does not mention.

## What the 15 posts say about Carry

1. **She puts time inside a still.** One body appears several times in one frame: the four falls in P05-01, the six fading copies in P10-01, the strip of poses in P09-01, the dancers beside her own pose in P14-16 and the face strip in P13-04. She thinks like an animator who publishes stills.
2. **She takes the ground color from the photograph.** P05-03 is a swatch card: grey, red, olive and blue squares taken from the sky, the hood, the top and the jeans. P05-04 to 06 then frame the photos in those same colors. P04, P06 and P10 use the same system: a window of photograph inside a flat field of a color found in the picture.
3. **She shows how a picture is built.** P01 runs from photograph to pencil to oil, with her own shadow drawing the line in P01-06. P07 runs from photograph to blue underdrawing to oil, then the oil on the studio wall. P12 shows sketches, wire, a light table and an outdoor easel.
4. **She performs inside other people’s art.** In P04-02 she copies the sculpture’s pose. In P06-08 she sits inside a mirrored yellow room. In P14-16 she dances in a gallery. In P15-03 she is small beside a church column.
5. **Three colors return constantly.** Red: the hood, the cap, the sculpture, the armchair, the basketball and the P04 fields. Ultramarine with orange or gold: Dusk til Dawn, the PHILA jersey, Giotto’s ceiling. Gold: the leaf on P08, the wire in P12-04 and the mosaics in P15. Black holds the night works (P02, P03, P13).
6. **Her friends are the subject as often as she is.** Most of P02, P03 and P13 is about the group: flash at night, costumes, fire, people crammed into one frame.
7. **She writes herself into the work.** “What should CARRY do?” sits under the pixelated Iverson 3 in P11-10. “To Carry Zheng” appears on the signed Curry biography in P07-12.

## The relationship: Domus Dei (P15) → Dusk til Dawn (P01)

Both works are ultramarine and gold on darkness. In P15-07, a gold halo holds a face inside a starred blue vault. In P01-02 the ear is painted as concentric orange rings, the same shape as the halo, and there is a thin silver ring at the mouth. White paint splatter fills the black above the head in P01-02 and P01-04, and it reads as a night sky.

### Opening A: halo → ear (6 s)

- **First frame:** P15-07 full-bleed. Giotto’s blue vault with gold stars and the glowing medallion at center.
- **0–2 s:** slow push into the medallion. The ring holds at center.
- **2–4 s:** the stars fade. The vault’s blue shifts to the ultramarine of the painted face and the black of the canvas comes up around it.
- **4–6 s:** the ring shrinks and slides left until it registers on the painted ear.
- **Last frame:** P01-02, with the ear exactly where the halo’s ring landed.
- **What changes:** a ring that frames a holy face becomes the ear of a friend glancing sideways. The blue stays. The icon becomes a person.

### Opening B: moon → paint (6 s)

- **First frame:** P15-06. The inset window of dusk shows a bell tower, a crane, the crescent moon and one star, set against the white and blue split field.
- **0–3 s:** the window holds while the surrounding field darkens from evening blue to black.
- **3–6 s:** the tower and crane drop away. The moon and the star stay in place and become flecks of white paint in the black above the painted head.
- **Last frame:** P01-04, the upper canvas with its white splatter.
- **What changes:** real light becomes paint, and dusk becomes the painting called Dusk til Dawn.

### The detail that resists

**P01-09, the source photograph, is daylight.** The subject sits in bleachers in a blue and white sports top, with hair blown across one eye. Nothing in it is nocturnal, sacred or gold. Carry invented the night in paint. Nothing in the archive says she had seen the Scrovegni or Assisi vaults when she painted P01. The rhyme between chapel and portrait is one **I** am drawing from palette and shape alone.

A second detail resists Opening B: **the crane in P15-06.** Carry kept a construction crane beside the bell tower. The same thing happens in P14-15 and P14-18, where stickers sit on old Italy. Her Italy is not purely reverent, so a clean “sacred → portrait” transition may flatten her.

## Choice and uncertainty

- **Choice:** Opening A. The ring is one shape carried across both works, so the eye can follow it without any explanation.
- **Uncertain:**
  - I don’t know whether the ring at the mouth in P01-01/02 is a hoop earring or a bubble.
  - I don’t know which work came first. If P15 is later, the transition runs against her own chronology.
  - I don’t know who the subject of P01 is.
