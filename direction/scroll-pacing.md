# Giving the pictures time

The live scroll travels about 83 screen heights. Eleven record-store pictures share 2.18 of those heights. A quick swipe can pass several pictures, even in a long scroll. The shape and colour handoffs give the story its rhythm. The pictures that carry the story need enough distance to register. [Opus’s sequence](scroll-direction.md)

## Three moments to screen

| Moment | What the next scroll adds |
| --- | --- |
| The oil portrait | Stay on its full rectangle while blue light edges the dark around it. Once the pencil wipe settles, hold the registered eye for a second look. The original photograph still follows. |
| The night opens into day | Stop on the two BLACK OUT prints at 47.8 s, then give the flame its own breath. Warm the ground around the halo and sculpture through the gold stretch. Once the four poses reach the waterfront in *Fly*, give the blue sky room before the next cut. |
| The studio | Let the paper unfold finish on the basketball photograph. Give Carry beside the canvas the longest hold on a phone before the portfolio’s final links appear. |

The ten holds add about 4.4 desktop screen heights or 7 phone screen heights in total. A hold freezes a chosen picture over a short distance of normal scrolling. The wall light rises and falls over that distance, behind the work. A fast scroll can pass straight through. Wider phone compositions keep the full oil portrait, the waterfront poses and the closing portrait readable around the edges of the stage.

The **Works** button opens fifteen original covers in an ordinary scrolling grid. Each opens Carry’s existing viewer with every slide, credits and the original source. Back from an artwork returns to the index. Back to story returns to the scene the visitor left. A shared `#works` link opens the grid directly.

Screen the new draft by asking a first-time viewer which two images they remember from the night-to-day stretch. Then ask them to find an individual project and return to the story. These two tasks test the change in rhythm and the speed of access.
