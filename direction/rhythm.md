# What the narration reference changes in a wordless film

The director shared a 20.48-second reel by @jordans.archivess. Its opening question finishes around 2.8 seconds. The answer arrives around 3.8. A record, camera, heart and game controller gather, separate and scatter. Near the end, the objects give way to widely spaced letters. The second of space after the question gives the viewer time to form an answer. Timing comes from the source audio and the visible captions. [Reference reel](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DeKDmkmh_gM/)

Carry’s film uses that rhythm in pictures. The opening eye changes from photograph to pencil to oil. The water drops away in *Fly* and the hooded poses hang long enough to recognize. Color blocks complete the descent. The clicks, metallic taps and bounce lead into a final 2.5 seconds in the studio. Hold that image so a viewer can discover the number 30 jersey beside the canvas. The director chose a wordless cut with an original stereo score and Foley. A future voice treatment would be a separate editorial decision with Carry.
