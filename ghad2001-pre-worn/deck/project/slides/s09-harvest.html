SPEAKER 2 · 4:15–4:55 “Cotton also raises a labour question. For years Uzbekistan’s government set cotton quotas and sent students, teachers, nurses and doctors into the fields every autumn, so clinics and schools emptied at harvest time. Forced labour means work under threat, without a real choice. Low pay alone is not forced labour. What ended it was collective: 331 brands refused to buy, and the ILO monitored every harvest. In 2021 it found no systemic forced labour, and the boycott was lifted in March 2022. Human Rights Watch says risks remain where crop targets remain. This is Park’s point that fairness makes collective action work.” We do not claim Levi’s bought Uzbek cotton.
