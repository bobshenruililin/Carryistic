SPEAKER 2 · 3:00–3:40 “This is Levi’s own life-cycle study of a 501. Each square’s area is a share. Two-thirds of the water goes into growing the cotton. For climate, the biggest single stage is us: washing and drying at home, 37 percent, which we come back to later. But notice what the study counts: water, carbon, energy. It has no row for lungs, or for wages. Park calls this the part and the whole: a narrow problem measured well, a wider one left out. The rest of this chapter is about that gap.” Check before Monday: the 68, 23, 37 and 9 percent figures against R04 itself (some secondary sources mix in the 2009 study), and that the study has no human-health category. “Rest” is our calculated remainder, labelled as such.
