SPEAKER 5 · 10:30–11:40 “How is it sold? In 1985 Levi’s filmed a young man stripping to his boxers to wash his 501s in a launderette. The film is credited with a huge sales rise; 800 percent is the figure usually quoted, though it is an estimate. In 2025 Levi’s remade the same scene with Beyoncé. Look at who each campaign targets: British teenagers in 1985; Gen Z in 2021; women, through Beyoncé; and Asia-Pacific fans through Rosé, with collectible photocards for Levi’s loyalty members. Then the twist: in 2021 Levi’s told Gen Z to ‘Buy Better, Wear Longer’, to buy less. Is that sustainability, or premium positioning? It can be both: fewer, pricier pairs can still grow revenue. The WHO calls marketing a commercial determinant of health: it shapes what feels normal to want. And like misinformation, an advert can be persuasive without being accurate.” Design note: Carry’s starburst over a strip of poses.
