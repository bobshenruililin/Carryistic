## 1. The assignment (from the brief, essentials verbatim)

## 2. The marker: Dr Andrew Park

He keeps things simple and rewards a story told well and *flows thinking*. Use his lens on every chapter:

- **Five questions of any flow:** what moves, by which route, who controls it, what conditions shape it, who benefits and who bears harm.

- **Claim → mechanism → example → implication, then a qualification.** Never end a chapter on a fact. End on what it changes about our understanding of globalisation, then add the counterargument or limit.

## 6. The argument (settled)

- **Thesis:** The jeans travel easily, as a price, a look and an idea. The wear is paid for in lungs, wages and drains, at exactly the stages where the record stops.
- **Criterion** (stated in the introduction and applied in each section): does the person who bears the health cost have a record of, or a say in, the decision that caused it?
- **Closing line:** "If value can travel across borders, why shouldn't responsibility?"
- **The rivet sequence, "How an idea travels"** (Ian's core; answers "why is the stitch that stopped a tear relevant?"):
  1. **Invention.** In 1871 a Reno tailor, Jacob Davis, rivets the pockets of a labourer's trousers. In his account the labourer was "sick with dropsy". US Patent 139,121 is granted on 20 May 1873.
  2. **Diffusion.** The patent expires (around 1890, verify) and the idea becomes everyone's. Don't claim expiry alone spread denim worldwide.
  3. **Reinterpretation.** Japanese craft denim, Korean pop culture, South Asian campaigns: the same garment given local meanings. Even the words travelled: jean from Genoa, denim from Nîmes, indigo from India to German synthetic in 1897.
  4. **Inversion.** A garment made to resist wear becomes one whose *apparent* destruction is desirable, from luxury "destroyed" denim down to a HK$600 pair.
  5. **The health link:** a cultural preference has a material consequence for someone else's lungs (back to Netanya's silicosis slide).
- **The pattern for every chapter:** evidence → mechanism → implication → qualification.
  - **Water:** water matters differently depending on local scarcity, not just litres.
  - **Labour:** jobs improved girls' schooling and autonomy while raising occupational risk.
  - **Bans:** a ban can move a hazard geographically or swap one risk for another.
  - **Care:** green care advice depends on housing, climate and floor space.
  - **Exports:** circularity counted inside one country's borders can hide costs incurred elsewhere.
