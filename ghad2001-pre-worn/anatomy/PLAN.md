# Teach Me Destructed: the anatomy plan

A new deck, built from scratch. The group's Google Slides draft gives us the **content**: speakers, research and notes. None of its look carries over, and none of Pre-Worn's either.

## 1. The idea in one picture

The whole talk is **one pair of jeans**, photographed like a museum object. Every part of the pair holds one thing the talk is about. Before each section the pair comes back as a map with labelled rectangles. Then we zoom into one part, and that part opens the section.

The pair is the outline, the progress bar and the argument at once:
- **The wearer's right leg** (the viewer's left) is the **making**. The pair is still new here: dark indigo, but with a factory-made rip at the knee.
- **The wearer's left leg** (the viewer's right) is the **wearing and the after**. It wears out as it goes down: a damp thigh, then a knee that was ripped and mended, then a hem that frays into loose threads.
- **Torn before it is worn:** both knees were ripped in a factory. The product name is "Teach Me Destructed". One rip stays raw, the evidence. The other is mended in copper thread, our suggestion.

## 2. The argument

**Thesis:** The jeans travel as a price and an idea. The wear is paid for in lungs, wages and drains, at exactly the stages where the record stops.

**Criterion** (said on slide 4, used in every section): does the person who bears the health cost have a record of, or a say in, the decision that caused it?

**Why the rivet belongs** (the question people asked about "the stitch that stopped a tear"): globalisation moves ideas as well as goods.
- Jacob Davis made the rivet for a labourer's trousers in 1871. The patent expired in 1890, and every maker in the world could copy it.
- The "worn look" is also an idea. It travelled from miners to luxury fashion to a HK$600 pair, and it is made by sanding, spraying or burning. So an idea travelling is what puts dust in a finisher's lungs.
- This connects the Usage section back to the Before section, and it is the course's "globalisation of ideas" theme made concrete. It is the slide where critical engagement is most visible.

**The scale tag** stays from the group draft: THIS PAIR / THIS BRAND / THIS SECTOR, plus GENERATED for AI images. Every slide says how close its evidence is to our pair.

## 4. Look and feel (from a blank page)

- **Photography:** hyper-real, like a museum or auction catalogue. One object on seamless pale cool-grey paper, soft top-left light, a real contact shadow. Every generated image is made from the same master pair, so the denim, the stitching and the light never change.
- **Ground colour:** sampled from the master image, so the slide and the photo are one surface. Section openers use deep indigo.
- **Type:**
  - Headlines in one tight grotesk; body text in a neutral sans.
  - Labels in a monospace that reads like a care tag.
  - Two weights. No italics, and no serif accents.
- **The map rectangles:** white tag-shaped boxes, a hairline border, a mono number and section name, and a thin leader line ending in a copper dot like a rivet. As the talk goes on, finished parts turn indigo and the current part lights copper.
- **Evidence:**
  - News and papers appear as **real screenshots** in one clipping frame, with the key line highlighted in copper.
  - Campaigns appear as real stills, credited.
  - AI images carry a GENERATED tag.
- **Density:** one idea per slide, one picture or one number. Speaker notes carry the rest.
- **Motion:** only the zoom from the map into a part (the same image, cropped), plus quick cuts in the montage. Nothing decorative.
- **The bench still applies:**
  - No cream paper, accent bars or italic serifs.
  - At most one "it's not X, it's Y" headline.
  - No generated real places, people or events.
  - None of the banned words.

## 5. Delivery and props

1. **The real pair** hangs at the front on a hanger. Each speaker touches their part as they start.
2. **The receipt** goes on every seat before class, with a short, plain front and the costs on the back (see props.md). Bob introduces it; Hanson has everyone turn it over.
3. **The map card** goes to Dr Park and the front row: a tag-shaped card with the pair and the five numbered parts, the agenda in their hands.
4. **The pocket moment:** "If you're wearing jeans, feel the corner of your front pocket" (slide 20).
5. **Handoffs** happen on the map: the outgoing speaker names the next part ("Megan takes us into the pocket").
