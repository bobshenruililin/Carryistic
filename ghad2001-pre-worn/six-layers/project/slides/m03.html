Hong Kong learned to wear masks from SARS, and ran out of them in 2020.

2003

SARS: 1,755 cases and 299 deaths in Hong Kong. Masks become a habit.

JAN–FEB 2020

Overnight queues. About 80% of masks sold here came from the mainland.

FEB 2020

The government seeks masks from 140 suppliers in 10 countries. Clinics close for lack of them.

MAY 2020

CuMask+: a reusable mask, designed here.

Department of Health (SARS) · AFP, 6 Feb 2020 · Hong Kong Government, 10 Feb 2020 · HKFP, 30 Jan 2020

SPEAKER 1 · 0:50–1:20 “Why masks, and why here? In 2003 SARS infected 1,755 people in Hong Kong and killed 299. Park’s guest lecture on the SARS hotel showed how travel and one local setting spread it. Masks became a Hong Kong habit. So when COVID arrived, people queued overnight. About 80 percent of the masks sold here came from the mainland, and supply stopped. The government went looking in ten countries, and some clinics closed because they had no masks. That is a supply chain failing in public. The government’s answer was a reusable mask designed in Hong Kong.” Check the SARS figures against the Department of Health page.
