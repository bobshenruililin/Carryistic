GHAD2001 · choosing the product · Saturday 28 November 2026

Which product should we follow?

Jeans are still a strong choice. The weak “two of eight stages” came from the particular pair we picked, not from jeans. If the group wants the most original and most course-aligned item and can get Dr Park’s approval quickly, the Hong Kong government’s CuMask+ beats every other option we looked at.

Highest ceiling

CuMask+, the copper mask every Hong Kong resident was offered in 2020

It is pandemic-era clothing, made in Hong Kong and recorded in LegCo papers: who designed it, who made it, where, at what cost and how many. It touches six of the course’s seven topics. The risks are approval (is a mask “clothing”?) and two days to rebuild.

Safest strong option

Levi’s 501, a version with a full production record

Levi’s own traceability site lists every production stage for mainstream 501s. Most of what we have built can stay, but the 501 has no pandemic strand of its own. We would add one: Levi’s 2020 payment decision and the year jeans lost out to sweatpants.

Why “two of eight” was our mistake

Our pair, PC9 005013599 “Teach Me Destructed”, is a Hong Kong-only style. Levi’s product-information site, built for EU disclosure rules, has no record of it. Mainstream 501s are recorded stage by stage, for each production order. We read these on 28 November:

The candidates

Each is scored from 1 to 5: how well it fits Park’s course, how traceable it is, how relevant to Hong Kong, how much pop culture and story it carries, and the risk of switching by Monday (5 means low risk).

1 · CuMask+ (HKRITA design; Hong Kong government, 2020)

  • A six-layer reusable mask with two copper layers, designed by the Hong Kong Research Institute of Textiles and Apparel. It cost about HK$40 each, and over 10 million had been given out by early 2021.
  • Made by Crystal International in Hong Kong and Vietnam, without a competitive tender. The official in charge admitted they had not disclosed the maker at first. That is our record thesis, in Hong Kong.
  • Course links: pathogens (SARS 2003, COVID, the SARS hotel lecture), infodemics (copper claims, mask debates), health knowledge (the patent and the design changes), corruption and transparency (the tender), pollution (masks washed up on the Soko Islands), and HK$40 of public money per person.
  • Pop culture: Hong Kong’s mask habit since SARS, the 945-day mandate (July 2020 to March 2023), masks as fashion, two million sign-ups in about a day. Most of us still have one in a drawer to photograph.
  • Risks: Dr Park may class it as medical rather than clothing, and it may overlap with a pharmaceuticals group.

3 · Mink fur (Danish mink, sold through the fur trade Hong Kong long served)

  • In November 2020 Denmark culled its entire farmed mink herd after a mink-linked virus variant, “Cluster 5”, was found in people. Mink on 290 of 1,147 farms had tested positive.
  • The strongest One Health story here, and fur is clearly clothing. Pelts are traceable through the auction system.
  • But few of us take a fur coat for granted, and the subject is heavy.
